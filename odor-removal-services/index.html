What this service involves

A bad smell is a symptom, not the problem itself — and air fresheners only bury it for a few hours. Professional odor removal starts with identifying the source: hidden mold behind drywall, a damp carpet pad, sewage residue in a drain line, or smoke particles embedded in porous materials. Until that source is addressed, no amount of deodorizing will make the smell stay gone.

Once the source is handled, providers use layered deodorization techniques matched to the odor. Musty and mildew smells respond to thorough drying, antimicrobial cleaning, and HEPA air scrubbers that capture airborne spores. Sewage and organic odors often need enzymatic cleaners that break down odor-causing compounds at the molecular level. Smoke and soot odors — common when a water event follows a small fire, or when HVAC systems spread smoke through a home — may call for thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatments that penetrate the same porous surfaces the odor penetrated.

The final step is verification: the provider confirms the smell is gone under normal conditions, not just masked by a strong deodorizer, and addresses any remaining problem areas. For severe cases, treatment may span multiple visits as odors release from deep within materials over time.

Signs you may need it

  • A musty or mildew smell that returns even after cleaning
  • Sewage or rotten-egg odors near drains, basements, or bathrooms
  • Smoke or soot smells lingering after a fire or furnace malfunction
  • Stale, damp odors in rooms that previously flooded or leaked
  • Odors that worsen when the HVAC system runs
  • Smells embedded in carpet, upholstery, or curtains after a water event

Common causes in Denver

Denver's seasonal flooding is the top driver of odor problems: basements that take on snowmelt or storm water and are dried incompletely develop musty smells within days, especially in finished basements with carpet and drywall. Sewer backups during heavy summer rains — a known issue in parts of the metro area — leave sewage odors that require professional neutralization, not just mopping.

Denver's wildfire smoke seasons add another dimension: when smoke infiltrates a home and is followed by fire-suppression water, the combination of soot and moisture bakes odors into drywall, insulation, and furnishings. Older Denver homes with aging drain lines and dry P-traps can also develop persistent sewer gas odors that homeowners mistake for a plumbing problem when it is really a sanitation and sealing issue. And in winter, when homes are sealed tight against the cold, any existing odor becomes far more noticeable — which is why musty-smell calls spike in the colder months.

Benefits of acting fast

Odors get harder — and more expensive — to remove the longer they sit, because odor molecules penetrate deeper into porous materials over time. Musty smells signal active mold growth, which can begin within 24 to 48 hours of water exposure and spreads the longer moisture remains. Fast source removal stops odors from embedding in drywall, insulation, and HVAC ductwork, where they become far more difficult to treat. Prompt action also protects indoor air quality for your household and keeps a minor smell from becoming the kind of problem that requires replacing carpet, pad, and drywall.

Cost considerations

We do not quote prices: odor removal cost depends on the odor type, its severity and spread, whether source materials must be removed, and which treatment methods are required. A light deodorization after a small clean-water leak costs far less than whole-home smoke or sewage odor remediation. A connected provider will assess the situation on-site and explain the recommended approach and cost before work begins.

When to call a professional

Call a professional when an odor persists after cleaning, when it is clearly linked to water damage, sewage, or smoke, or when you can smell it but cannot find its source — hidden mold and contaminated cavities are the usual culprits. Call immediately for sewage odors, which indicate a sanitation issue, and for strong chemical or gas-like smells, which may signal a safety hazard. If the odor worsens when your heating or cooling runs, professional help is warranted, since the HVAC system may be spreading contaminants.

Frequently asked questions

Can you really remove musty odors permanently?

Yes, when the odor source is addressed. Deodorizing without removing the damp material or mold causing the smell only masks it temporarily. Providers find and eliminate the source first.

What methods are used for odor removal?

Providers use a combination of source removal, cleaning and sanitizing, HEPA air scrubbers, thermal fogging, and hydroxyl or ozone treatments depending on the odor type and severity.

Are odor removal treatments safe for pets and kids?

Most deodorization methods are safe when applied by professionals following label directions. Some treatments, like ozone, require the space to be unoccupied during application — your provider will explain any precautions.

How long does odor removal take?

Simple deodorization can be completed in a day, while severe or widespread odors may need multiple treatments over several days. The provider assesses severity on-site.
