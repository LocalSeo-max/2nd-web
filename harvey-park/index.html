Water damage risks in Harvey Park

  • Aging supply lines and water heaters. Plumbing components with many years of service face the general risks of corrosion and fatigue — a Denver-wide pattern in mid-century and older housing.
  • Slow hidden leaks. Pinhole leaks and dripping connections can soak framing and subfloors for months before staining appears, making moisture inspection especially valuable here.
  • Spring snowmelt seepage. Basements take on meltwater through foundation cracks and floor joints each spring, particularly after heavy snow years.
  • Frozen pipes in winter. Crawlspaces, garages, and exterior-wall plumbing can freeze during deep cold snaps and burst on the thaw.
  • Summer storm runoff. Intense rainfall can overwhelm gutters and grading, pushing water into window wells and against foundations.

What should I do before the provider arrives?

Shut off the water source if you can, turn off power to wet areas, move furniture and valuables, and photograph the damage. Avoid wading into water that may be contaminated or near electricity.

Does insurance cover gradual pipe leaks?

Most policies distinguish sudden events (usually covered) from gradual leaks (often excluded). Providers we connect you with document the damage and timeline, which helps your insurer assess the claim.
