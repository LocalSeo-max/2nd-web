What this service involves

Not all water is equal. Clean water from a supply line mainly needs drying, but gray water from dishwashers, washing machines, and sump failures — and black water from sewage backups and floodwater — carries bacteria, viruses, and other pathogens that make surfaces unsafe. Professional sanitization begins with removing contaminated water and any porous materials that cannot be safely disinfected, such as soaked carpet pad, wet insulation, and heavily affected drywall.

Providers then clean all affected hard surfaces to remove organic matter — disinfectants cannot work through layers of grime — and apply EPA-registered antimicrobial products at the correct concentration and dwell time. This is a precision step: spraying a store-bought cleaner and wiping it off immediately does not achieve disinfection. For widespread contamination, providers may use fogging equipment to reach wall cavities, subfloors, and other areas that cannot be wiped by hand.

The process concludes with verification that treated areas are clean and dry, and documentation of the products and methods used. In commercial settings such as restaurants, daycares, and healthcare-adjacent spaces, providers follow stricter protocols appropriate to the facility's use, and they can advise on any required clearance steps before reopening.

Signs you may need it

  • Water from a sewage backup, toilet overflow, or drain backup entered your space
  • Floodwater or storm runoff came inside the building
  • Water sat stagnant for more than a day or two before cleanup
  • Foul or rotten odors persist after water was removed
  • Visible slime, discoloration, or residue on surfaces after drying
  • A daycare, restaurant, rental, or healthcare space was affected by any water event

Common causes in Denver

Denver's heavy summer storms are the leading contamination risk: intense downpours overwhelm municipal sewers and cause backups into basements and ground-floor bathrooms, while street flooding pushes contaminated runoff into homes and businesses. Spring snowmelt saturates soils and can drive groundwater — carrying soil-borne contaminants — through foundation cracks into lower levels.

Dishwasher and washing machine overflows, common in Denver's busy households, release gray water laden with food waste and detergents that breeds bacteria quickly in warm indoor conditions. In older Denver neighborhoods, aging clay sewer laterals crack and allow root intrusion, setting up recurring backups. And because many Denver homes sit vacant during ski-season getaways, a leak that runs for days while no one is home can turn initially clean water into a bacterial problem — which is why a prompt professional inspection matters after any unattended leak.

Benefits of acting fast

Bacteria multiply rapidly in warm, wet environments — and mold can begin growing on damp materials within 24 to 48 hours, adding a second contamination problem on top of the first. Fast professional response limits how far contaminated water spreads into wall cavities, subfloors, and HVAC systems, where it becomes far harder to treat. Prompt disinfection also protects your household's health, prevents odors from embedding in porous materials, and keeps porous belongings — furniture, rugs, stored items — from becoming unsalvageable. For businesses, speed means reopening sooner with proper documentation of the sanitation work.

Cost considerations

We never invent prices: sanitization and disinfection cost depends on the contamination level, the size of the affected area, how much porous material must be removed and discarded, and whether specialized equipment like foggers or air scrubbers is needed. Black-water events cost more to remediate than clean-water events because of the protective procedures and material disposal involved. A provider will assess on-site and explain the scope and cost before work begins.

When to call a professional

Call a professional for any sewage backup, toilet overflow beyond a minor clean-water spill, floodwater intrusion, or water that has sat stagnant for more than a day. Do not attempt DIY cleanup of black water — it requires protective equipment and proper disposal procedures. Also call if anyone in the household has been exposed to contaminated water and surfaces need verification, or if a commercial space such as a restaurant or daycare was affected, where sanitation standards are higher.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between sanitizing and disinfecting?

Sanitizing reduces the number of germs on a surface to a safe level; disinfecting kills a higher percentage of pathogens. Providers choose the level appropriate to the contamination involved.

When is disinfection needed after water damage?

Disinfection is needed when water may be contaminated — sewage backups, drain overflows, floodwater, or water that sat long enough for bacteria to grow. Clean supply-line leaks typically need cleaning rather than full disinfection.

Are the chemicals used safe for my family?

Professional providers use EPA-registered products applied according to label directions, which include dwell times and ventilation requirements. Your provider will explain any precautions for children, pets, and food-contact surfaces.

Does sanitization remove mold?

Sanitizing kills surface microorganisms but does not remove established mold growth or its root structures. Visible mold requires dedicated mold remediation.

Do I need to leave my home during disinfection?

Usually not for standard antimicrobial treatments. For heavy fogging or certain chemical applications, the provider may ask you to vacate the treated area until it is ventilated.
