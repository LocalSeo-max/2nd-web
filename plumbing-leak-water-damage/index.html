Plumbing Leak Water Damage Repair in Denver, CO

Not all plumbing disasters announce themselves. Slow leaks — a dripping supply line inside a wall, a failing toilet seal, a weeping shower valve — can soak subfloors and framing for months before the first stain appears. What This Service Involves

Plumbing leak jobs start with detective work. Because the leak is often hidden, the provider uses moisture detection — thermal imaging and moisture meters — to map how far water traveled and trace it to the source: a supply line, drain joint, toilet base, or shower pan. Only with that map does the real work begin.

From there, the process mirrors a careful restoration: the water supply is stopped, wet materials are assessed, and structural drying equipment pulls moisture from framing, subfloors, and wall cavities. What can't be saved — rotted subfloor, mold-eaten drywall, delaminated cabinetry — is removed and replaced, then finishes are restored. Providers in our trusted Denver restoration services network know the particular challenge of leak jobs: the damage is often worse than it looks, because time was on the water's side.

Signs You May Need It

  • A water bill that rises with no change in household usage
  • The sound of running or dripping water when no fixture is on
  • Musty odors under sinks, behind toilets, or near the water heater
  • Warped, buckling, or discolored flooring near plumbing fixtures
  • Cabinets or vanities with swollen, stained, or soft bottoms
  • Stains on ceilings below bathrooms, kitchens, or laundry rooms
  • Warm spots on a slab floor or cracks with dampness (possible slab leak)

Common Causes in Denver

Denver's housing stock gives plumbing leaks plenty of opportunity. Aging supply lines and corroded galvanized pipe weep at joints for years. Toilet wax rings and supply connectors fail silently, wetting the subfloor with every flush. Shower pans develop hairline cracks that drip into wall cavities with every shower. Winter freeze-thaw stress loosens fittings that then drip all year. Slab foundations hide under-slab line leaks that surface as warm spots. And Denver's mineral-heavy water accelerates corrosion, shortening pipes' leak-free lifespan. Benefits of Acting Fast

With slow leaks, "fast" means acting on the first clue, not the tenth. Mold can begin growing on damp materials within 24 to 48 hours of wetting — and in a hidden leak, that clock has been running for weeks. Every month converts more salvageable material into tear-out: dryable subfloor becomes replaceable subfloor, and a contained mold patch becomes a wall-cavity colony needing mold remediation. Cost Considerations

Plumbing leak repair cost hinges on two unknowns only an inspection resolves: how long the leak ran, and how far water traveled. A recent under-sink leak is modest; a shower pan leak that ran a year behind tile can mean subfloor replacement and mold remediation. Your provider will trace the leak, assess on-site, and explain scope and pricing before work begins.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if I have a hidden plumbing leak?

Watch for a water bill that climbs without explanation, the sound of running water when nothing is on, musty smells, warped flooring, or stains that grow slowly. A professional with moisture meters and thermal imaging can confirm a hidden leak and trace it to the source.

What's the difference between a plumbing leak and a burst pipe?

A burst pipe releases a large volume of water suddenly at full pressure. A plumbing leak releases water slowly over days or months — often hidden inside walls or under floors — giving moisture time to feed mold and rot wood.

Can a slow leak really cause serious damage?

Yes — slow leaks are responsible for some of the worst damage providers see, precisely because they go unnoticed. Months of steady dripping can rot subfloors, destroy cabinetry, saturate insulation, and grow extensive mold behind walls.

Do you fix the leaking pipe itself?

Water damage providers focus on the damage the leak caused: finding the moisture, drying the structure, removing ruined materials, and restoring the area. The pipe repair is plumbing work, which the provider can coordinate with a plumber — ask when you call.

What are the most common plumbing leak locations in a home?

Under sinks, behind toilets (wax ring and supply line), inside shower and tub walls, at washing machine and dishwasher connections, water heater bases, and slab foundations where pipes run under concrete. Anywhere water connections live is worth checking periodically.
