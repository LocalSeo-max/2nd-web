What This Service Involves

Frozen pipe jobs follow a signature pattern. The freeze usually happens overnight in an exposed run — an exterior wall, crawl space, or attic — and the burst is discovered at the thaw, often as water pouring through a ceiling. The response starts with stopping the flow at the main valve, then water extraction removes standing water before it migrates further.

Winter jobs are tricky to dry: cold air holds less moisture, so structural drying must be managed carefully, sometimes with added heat. Providers also check the full extent of freeze damage — one split pipe often means others in the same run are stressed. As winter work within Denver's water damage referral network, these jobs get emergency priority, since a thaw burst discovered late can soak a home for hours unnoticed.

Signs You May Need It

  • No water (or just a trickle) from a faucet during freezing weather
  • Frost visible on exposed pipes, or a pipe that looks swollen
  • Water appearing as temperatures rise — the classic thaw burst
  • Damp spots, dripping, or staining on ceilings below bathrooms or kitchens
  • Pooling water in a crawl space, basement, or garage after a cold snap
  • The sound of running water inside walls when no fixture is on
  • A sudden drop in water pressure across the house in winter

Common Causes in Denver

Denver's freeze-thaw rhythm is the root cause. Arctic air drops temperatures fast, freezing pipes in exterior walls, unheated crawl spaces, attics, and garages — especially in older, poorly insulated homes. The danger spikes when travelers set the heat very low or a furnace fails mid-snap. Hose bibbs left connected freeze back into the wall. And the thaw is the cruel part: pressure behind the ice finds the split the moment water flows again — which is why so many bursts surface on the first warm afternoon after a deep freeze. Frequently Asked Questions

Why do frozen pipes burst when they thaw, not when they freeze?

The ice blockage itself usually doesn't split the pipe — the pressure buildup between the ice and a closed faucet does, often at a weak point downstream. When the ice thaws, full pressure returns and water escapes through the split. That's why many bursts are discovered during the thaw.

My pipe is frozen but hasn't burst. What should I do?

Keep the faucet open, apply gentle heat (warm towels, a hair dryer on low — never an open flame or torch), and watch for leaks as it thaws, since the pipe may have split. If you're unsure, shut the main valve before thawing so a hidden split can't flood the house.

Which pipes freeze most often in Denver homes?

Pipes in exterior walls, unheated crawl spaces and attics, garages, and hose bibbs are the usual victims — especially during hard cold snaps or when a home's heat is set very low or fails during travel.

Can you help in the middle of a cold snap, or do I have to wait?

Call immediately — providers in our network respond to winter emergencies around the clock, so extraction and drying start before damage compounds.

How do I keep pipes from freezing next winter?

Insulate exposed pipes, seal air leaks near them, keep the thermostat steady (even when away), open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during extreme cold, and disconnect outdoor hoses before the first freeze.
