Water damage risks in Capitol Hill

  • Multi-unit water migration. In apartments and condos, one leak can damage several units. Water follows gravity through ceilings and chases along pipes, widening the affected area quickly.
  • Aging building plumbing. Older residential buildings carry the general risks of corroded supply lines, failing shutoff valves, and tired drain stacks — a common Denver-wide pattern in long-standing housing.
  • Flat and low-slope roof leaks. Many central Denver buildings have flat or low-slope roofs where ponding water exploits worn membranes and flashing during storms and snowmelt.
  • Frozen pipes in winter. Pipes in exterior walls, unheated stairwells, and vacant units can freeze and burst during prolonged cold.
  • Fixture and appliance overflows. Overflowing toilets, failed washing machine hoses, and leaking water heaters are frequent sources in dense residential buildings.
