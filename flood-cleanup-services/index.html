Flood Cleanup Services in Denver, CO

Flood cleanup is the hands-on first phase of flood recovery: getting floodwater out, clearing away the mud, silt, and debris it carried in, and cleaning and sanitizing everything it touched. It doesn't include rebuilding — that's the restoration phase that comes later. What this service involves

Flood cleanup is methodical, physical work, and it follows a deliberate order. First, the crew ensures the space is safe to enter — checking for electrical hazards, structural concerns like compromised ceilings or floors, and contamination levels. Then standing floodwater is pumped and extracted out. What makes flood cleanup distinct from a clean-water job is what comes next: the residue. Floodwater from outside is treated as contaminated, so crews shovel and clear mud, silt, and debris; remove waterlogged porous materials that can't be sanitized (carpet padding, soaked drywall sections, insulation); and then wash down hard surfaces. Disinfection follows — antimicrobial treatments on surfaces that will remain — and finally, drying equipment is set up so the space can begin the drying phase with a clean slate.

Signs you may need flood cleanup

  • Floodwater has entered your home from outside — streets, yards, or overflowing drainage
  • Mud, silt, sand, or debris is coating floors and lower walls
  • A visible water line marks how high the flood reached on walls and furniture
  • The water or the space smells foul, musty, or like sewage
  • Flooring is lifting, warping, or squelching across wide areas
  • Belongings, boxes, and stored items are soaked or mud-caked
  • Water has receded on its own but left contamination and dampness behind

Common causes in Denver

Denver's floods tend to be sudden and localized rather than the slow river rises seen elsewhere. Cloudburst thunderstorms in summer can overwhelm storm drains in minutes, pushing brown water across streets and into ground-level spaces. Rapid spring snowmelt swells creeks and irrigation ditches; properties near waterways or at the bottom of a slope take the hit. Hailstorms compound the problem by damaging roofs and breaking basement windows right before the rain arrives. In some areas, aging or undersized drainage infrastructure simply can't handle peak flows, so water backs up through street drains and into homes. And don't overlook the winter variant: when snow piles against foundations melt during a warm snap, water finds every crack and window well it can. Whatever brought the water in, our network of Denver water damage pros handles the messy aftermath.

What happens when you call

  2. We connect you with a local provider. You're matched with an independent Denver-area provider experienced in contaminated-water cleanup, not just clean-water drying.
  3. Safety assessment first. The crew checks for electrical hazards and structural issues before anyone starts work, and sets up containment so contaminants don't spread to dry areas.
  4. Cleanup begins. Water is pumped and extracted, mud and debris are cleared, unsalvageable materials are removed, and remaining surfaces are washed and disinfected.
  5. Drying starts and next steps are explained. Air movers and dehumidifiers go in, and the provider outlines the drying timeline plus whether repairs will be needed afterward.

Benefits of acting fast

With floodwater, the clock matters for a specific reason: contamination. Floodwater from outside carries sewage, chemicals, and bacteria, and the longer it sits, the deeper those contaminants penetrate porous materials — turning items that might have been cleaned into items that must be thrown away. Fast cleanup also limits how far water migrates into wall cavities and subfloors, where it becomes much harder to reach. Mold can begin within 24 to 48 hours in saturated materials, and flood-soaked drywall and insulation are ideal hosts. There's a documentation benefit too: the sooner a professional crew is on site, the more complete the record of damage for insurance purposes. Calling quickly for water removal and cleanup gives you the smallest possible job and the best possible outcome.

Cost considerations

We don't set prices — flood cleanup cost depends on the area affected, the depth and contamination level of the water, the volume of debris and mud, how much material must be removed and disposed of, and the drying time that follows. A garage that took on a few inches and a finished basement under a foot of muddy water are very different projects. Your provider will assess the property in person and explain pricing before work begins. Because floodwater is treated as contaminated, cleanup involves more labor, protective measures, and disposal than a clean-water job — your provider can walk you through exactly what's included. Document everything for insurance: standard homeowners policies often exclude outside flooding, so knowing your coverage matters.

When to call a professional

Any time floodwater from outside enters your home, call a professional — this isn't a DIY cleanup. Contaminated water is a health hazard: don't wade through it, don't let children or pets near it, and don't try to clean it with household products. Call immediately if water is near electrical panels or outlets, if there's a sewage smell, or if ceilings or floors look structurally compromised. Even after water recedes on its own, the contamination and hidden moisture remain — professional cleanup plus sanitization is the only way to make the space truly safe again. If you're unsure whether what you're seeing qualifies as flooding, describe it on the call and let the provider advise you.

Frequently asked questions

Is flood cleanup the same as flood damage restoration?

No. Cleanup is the first phase — removing floodwater, mud, debris, and sanitizing surfaces. Restoration is the complete process, which includes cleanup plus drying, repairs, and rebuilding. If your home needs repairs after the cleanup, ask your provider about full flood damage restoration.

Is floodwater dangerous to touch?

Assume it is. Floodwater from outside is treated as contaminated — it can carry sewage, chemicals, and bacteria. Avoid contact, keep children and pets away, and let professionals with proper protective equipment handle the cleanup.

Can anything be saved after a flood?

Hard, non-porous items — metal, glass, hard plastics, solid wood furniture — can often be cleaned and disinfected. Porous materials like carpet padding, soaked drywall, mattresses, and upholstered furniture usually can't be safely salvaged after contact with contaminated floodwater. The crew will help you sort what's worth keeping.

How do I get rid of the smell after a flood?

Odor comes from contaminants and moisture trapped in materials. Professional cleaning, disinfection, thorough drying, and removal of unsalvageable materials eliminate the source. If odor lingers after everything is dry, dedicated odor removal services can address what remains.
