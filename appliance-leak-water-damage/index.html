What this service involves

Appliance leaks are deceptive: the water you can see pooling on the kitchen tile is rarely the full story. Dishwashers and washing machines discharge large volumes of water that run under cabinets, behind baseboards, and into wall cavities and subfloors — areas that stay damp long after the floor looks dry. Professional cleanup starts by confirming the appliance's water supply is off, then extracting standing water and pulling back toe kicks, baseboards, and, when necessary, cabinet bases to reach the trapped moisture.

Local providers in our network use moisture meters and infrared checks to map how far water traveled through subflooring and drywall, then place air movers and dehumidifiers in the right configuration to dry those hidden zones. Cabinetry made of particle board often swells and delaminates when wet and may need partial replacement; hardwood floors may need specialty drying to prevent cupping. Once materials test dry, providers handle repairs such as drywall patches, baseboard replacement, and flooring restoration, so the kitchen or laundry room is returned to normal.

Because dishwasher and washing machine water can carry food residue, detergents, or soil, providers also clean and sanitize affected surfaces — a step that keeps odors from developing and prepares surfaces for repair. Throughout the process they document conditions with photos and moisture readings, which is useful if you open a homeowners insurance claim.

Signs you may need it

  • Standing water or a spreading puddle near a dishwasher, washer, or refrigerator
  • Warped, buckled, or discolored flooring around an appliance
  • Swollen, bubbling, or peeling cabinetry or vanities near a leak source
  • Damp or soft drywall behind or beside an appliance
  • A musty or sour odor in the kitchen or laundry room
  • Water stains appearing on the ceiling or wall below an upstairs laundry room

Common causes in Denver

Washing machine supply hoses are a leading cause — rubber hoses age and burst, especially in Denver homes with higher water pressure or homes built before braided steel hoses were standard. Dishwasher leaks often come from worn door seals, loose drain connections, or cracked tubs, and because dishwashers cycle on timers, they can leak for an entire cycle while no one is home. Refrigerator ice-maker lines, typically thin plastic or copper tubing, crack or work loose behind the fridge where the leak goes unnoticed until the hardwood swells.

Denver's hard water is another local factor: mineral buildup wears on appliance valves, pumps, and seals faster than in soft-water regions, shortening the life of connectors and gaskets. In upstairs laundry rooms common in newer Denver builds, a failed washer hose or pump sends water down through floor cavities into the ceiling below, turning one appliance failure into a two-room problem.

Benefits of acting fast

Appliance leaks often hide under cabinets and inside walls, where mold can begin growing on damp drywall and wood within 24 to 48 hours. Acting fast limits how far water migrates through subfloors and into adjacent rooms, which is what keeps a manageable kitchen cleanup from becoming a whole-floor project. Quick extraction also protects hardwood and laminate floors from permanent warping and keeps particle-board cabinets from swelling beyond repair — in many cases, the difference between drying a cabinet in place and replacing it comes down to a matter of hours.

Cost considerations

We never invent prices: the cost of appliance leak cleanup depends on how much water was released, how many rooms or cavities were affected, whether cabinetry or flooring must be replaced, and the water's category (a clean supply-line leak versus a drain-line backup). A local provider will assess the damage on-site, take moisture readings, and explain your options and costs before any work begins. Ask about documentation for your insurance claim, since sudden appliance failures are frequently covered by homeowners policies.

When to call a professional

Call when an appliance has released enough water to pool on the floor, soak into cabinets or flooring, or reach the wall — especially if water is still flowing or the source is unclear. Call immediately if the leak is coming from a drain line rather than a supply line, if water has spread under flooring or into a wall, or if the appliance is on an upper floor where water may be affecting the rooms below. A musty smell days after a small leak is also worth a professional check, since it often means hidden moisture was missed.

Frequently asked questions

What appliances most commonly cause water damage?

Washing machines, dishwashers, refrigerators with ice makers, and water softeners are the most common culprits. Supply hoses, drain lines, and internal seals fail over time.

What should I do first when an appliance is leaking?

Turn off the appliance's water supply valve, shut off the appliance, and unplug it if it is safe to do so. Then move valuables and call for professional cleanup.

Can appliance leak water cause mold?

Yes. Appliance leaks often soak subfloors, cabinetry, and wall cavities that stay hidden, and mold can begin growing on damp materials within 24 to 48 hours.

Will my insurance cover appliance leak damage?

Many homeowners policies cover sudden appliance leaks, though coverage varies and gradual leaks may be excluded. The restoration provider can document the damage to support your claim.
