Water damage risks in Green Valley Ranch

  • Summer storms and roof leaks. Heavy Front Range thunderstorms can drive rain under flashing and shingles, and clogged gutters can send water sheeting down siding or pooling against the foundation.
  • Yard drainage and grading issues. Flat or poorly graded lots can channel snowmelt and stormwater toward the home, leading to seepage into lower levels and window wells.
  • Winter pipe freezes. Pipes in garages, exterior walls, and unheated spaces can freeze and burst during extended cold snaps, releasing a large volume of water fast.
  • Appliance failures. Washing machine supply hoses, dishwasher leaks, and refrigerator ice-maker lines are among the most common causes of indoor water damage in suburban homes.
  • Sewer backups during heavy rain. Intense storms can overwhelm storm drains and laterals, sending contaminated water back up through floor drains and lower-level bathrooms.

What should I do first when I find water damage in my home?

Stop the water source if you safely can (shut off the main valve for plumbing leaks), turn off electricity to affected rooms, move valuables off wet floors, and take photos for your insurer. Then call (303) 279-8078 and we will connect you with a local provider.

Will my homeowners insurance cover the damage?

Many homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as burst pipes or appliance failures, while gradual leaks and groundwater seepage are often excluded. The independent providers we connect you with can document the damage and provide the paperwork insurers typically ask for.

What affects the cost of water damage restoration?
