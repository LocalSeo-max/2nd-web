Water Damage Risks in Washington Park

Storm Damage to Roofs

Summer thunderstorms bring hail, high winds, and falling branches that damage shingles, flashing, and gutters. Once the roof envelope is breached, rainwater pours into attics and down through ceilings and walls.

Aging Supply Lines

As a general risk in older housing stock, aging pipes, valves, and connectors corrode and weaken over the decades. When a supply line fails, pressurized water floods the surrounding rooms fast.

Clogged Gutters and Downspouts

Heavy tree cover means gutters fill with leaves and debris. Overflowing gutters send water cascading down exterior walls and pooling at the foundation, where it seeps into basements.

Water Heater and Appliance Leaks

Water heaters, washing machines, and dishwashers all fail eventually. In multi-level homes, a leak on an upper floor damages ceilings, walls, and flooring on every level below.

Snowmelt Basement Seepage

Spring thaws push groundwater against foundations. Older concrete and mortar joints give way to seepage that dampens basements and finished lower levels for weeks.

Washington Park Water Damage FAQ

A storm just damaged my roof and water is coming in. What do I do?

Contain what you can — buckets, towels, moving valuables — and call (303) 279-8078. Providers can arrange emergency mitigation like tarping coordination and interior water extraction to limit damage until permanent roof repairs happen.

There's a brown stain on my ceiling but it's dry now. Should I still call?

Yes. A dry stain means water got in before and may again — and moisture can linger inside the ceiling cavity, feeding mold. An inspection can confirm whether the area is truly dry and where the water entered.

How do I know if there's mold in my walls?

Musty odors, visible discoloration, and allergy-like symptoms that improve away from home are clues, but only a professional inspection can confirm it. If mold is found, providers offer dedicated remediation.

Will my claim be denied if I wait too long to call?

Insurers generally expect prompt action to mitigate damage, and long delays can complicate a claim. Calling quickly and documenting everything gives your claim the strongest footing.
