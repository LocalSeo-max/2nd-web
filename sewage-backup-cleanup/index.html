Sewage Backup Cleanup in Denver, CO

A sewage backup is one of the most unpleasant — and genuinely hazardous — things that can happen in a home. When wastewater flows backward through floor drains, toilets, or tubs, it brings bacteria and pathogens with it, and cleanup requires protective equipment, containment, and professional-grade disinfection. This is not a DIY job. What this service involves

Sewage backup cleanup follows a strict protocol because the water involved — classified as black water — is a health hazard. Crews arrive in protective gear: respirators, gloves, boots, and coveralls. The first step is containment — sealing off the affected area so contaminants don't travel through the house on air currents or foot traffic. Then the sewage itself is extracted using dedicated equipment that never touches clean-water jobs. Everything porous that contacted sewage has to go: carpet and padding, soaked drywall, insulation, upholstered items, mattresses. There's no salvaging these — disinfection can't reliably penetrate porous materials.

Signs you may need sewage backup cleanup

  • Wastewater backing up through floor drains, toilets, tubs, or showers
  • Multiple drains gurgling or draining slowly at the same time
  • A strong sewage odor coming from drains or the basement
  • Dark, murky water with visible waste or toilet paper
  • Toilets that bubble or overflow when other fixtures drain
  • Damp, foul-smelling areas around floor drains after heavy rain
  • Any water you suspect came from a sewer line — when in doubt, treat it as sewage

Common causes in Denver

Denver's sewage backups have a few familiar triggers. Heavy rain is the biggest: intense downpours can overwhelm combined or aging sewer infrastructure, pushing wastewater backward into homes through the lowest drains — which is why backups so often appear in basements first. Tree root intrusion is a year-round problem; roots seek out the moisture in sewer laterals and gradually choke them, a common issue in Denver's older neighborhoods with mature trees and aging clay or cast-iron lines. Blockages from grease, wipes (even "flushable" ones), and other non-dispersing materials build up until the line can't pass flow. In some areas, municipal main problems during peak flows send backups into multiple homes on the same street. And Denver's freeze-thaw ground movement can shift or crack older laterals, creating bellies and offsets where waste collects. If backups keep recurring, local water damage restoration in Denver providers can document the pattern while a plumber addresses the line itself.

What happens when you call

  2. We connect you with a local provider. You're matched with an independent Denver-area provider experienced in black-water cleanup with the right protective equipment.
  3. Containment and assessment. The crew seals off the affected area, evaluates the extent of contamination, and identifies the likely source of the backup.
  4. Extraction, removal, and disinfection. Sewage is extracted, contaminated porous materials are removed for disposal, and all remaining surfaces are professionally cleaned and disinfected.
  5. Drying and next-step guidance. Drying equipment runs until the space is verified dry, and the provider explains what caused the backup and what to address — plumbing repair, backwater valve, line cleaning — to prevent a repeat.

Benefits of acting fast

With sewage, speed is about health as much as property. Bacteria and pathogens multiply in standing wastewater, and every hour of exposure increases the contamination of porous materials — acting fast is what determines whether hard surfaces can simply be disinfected or whether the damage spreads further into the home. Sewage also produces gases and odors that make more of the house unlivable the longer it sits, and it can begin damaging finishes and belongings immediately. Fast professional response contains the affected area before foot traffic or HVAC airflow spreads contamination to clean rooms. And because mold can begin within 24 to 48 hours in the damp aftermath, prompt cleanup plus drying prevents a sewage problem from becoming a sewage-plus-mold problem. Don't wait to see if it gets worse — with sewage, it will.

Cost considerations

We never quote prices: sewage backup cleanup cost depends on the extent of the backup, how many areas are affected, how much contaminated material must be removed and disposed of, and the disinfection and drying time required. Because it involves protective protocols, specialized disposal, and thorough sanitizing, it is more labor-intensive than a clean-water job — your provider will explain exactly what's included when they assess the property. When to call a professional

Always — for any sewage backup, no matter how small it looks. There is no safe level of DIY sewage cleanup: without proper protective equipment and disinfection products, you risk serious illness, and household cleaning can't reliably sanitize what sewage has touched. Call immediately and stay out of the affected area; keep children and pets away completely. If sewage is near electrical equipment, mention it on the call so the crew plans accordingly. Even a backup that seems to have drained away on its own leaves contamination behind — professional sanitization is the only way to make the space safe again. This is the clearest "call a professional" scenario in all of water damage work.

Frequently asked questions

Is a sewage backup dangerous?

Yes. Sewage is classified as black water — it can carry bacteria, viruses, and other pathogens that cause serious illness. Stay out of the affected area, keep children and pets away, and let professionals with proper protective equipment handle the cleanup. Don't try to clean it yourself.

Should I stay in my home during sewage cleanup?

It's best to leave the affected area until cleanup and sanitizing are complete. Whether the rest of the home is safe to occupy depends on the extent of the backup and your home's layout — your provider can advise once they've assessed the situation.

Can anything be saved after a sewage backup?

Hard, non-porous items — metal, glass, hard plastics, solid-surface countertops — can usually be professionally cleaned and disinfected. Porous materials that contacted sewage — carpet, padding, upholstered furniture, mattresses, soaked drywall and insulation — generally must be removed and discarded. The crew will help you sort through belongings.

Does homeowners insurance cover sewage backups?

Standard policies often exclude sewer backups unless you've added specific sewer backup coverage — check your policy documents. Thorough documentation from your cleanup provider, including photos and lists of removed materials, supports any claim you file. Lingering odor after the job is done can be addressed with odor removal services.
