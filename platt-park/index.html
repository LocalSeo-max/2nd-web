Water Damage Risks in Platt Park

Winter Pipe Freezes

Platt Park's older cottages and bungalows often have plumbing in crawl spaces, exterior walls, and unheated additions. Denver's deep freezes split these lines; the flood arrives with the thaw, sometimes while the house is quiet.

Spring Snowmelt Seepage

As snowpack melts, groundwater rises against foundations. Older concrete and aging waterproofing let seepage through cracks and joints, dampening basements and crawl spaces each spring.

Aging Plumbing

As a general risk in older housing stock, aging supply lines, shutoff valves, and connectors corrode over time. A failure behind a wall or under a floor can soak framing long before it shows.

Appliance Leaks

Washing machines, dishwashers, and water heaters fail with little warning. In smaller homes, even a moderate leak affects a large share of the living space.

Roof Leaks in Storms

Hail and wind-driven summer rain find worn shingles and tired flashing. Water entering the roofline travels inside walls and appears as stains far from the entry point.

Platt Park Water Damage FAQ

Which rooms are most at risk from a burst pipe in winter?

Rooms with plumbing on exterior walls — kitchens, bathrooms, and laundry areas — plus basements and crawl spaces where pipes run unheated. Finished basements suffer the most damage because water spreads across flooring and into wall cavities.

My basement wall feels damp but there's no standing water. Is that a problem?

Yes, it can be. Persistent dampness feeds mold and deteriorates materials over time. A professional moisture assessment can determine whether it's condensation, minor seepage, or something needing active drying.

Should I turn off my water main if I find a leak?

If water is flowing and you know where the main shutoff is, yes — stopping the flow limits damage. Then call for help. If you can't find the shutoff safely, call us first and we'll guide you while connecting you with a provider.
