Water Damage Risks in University

Basement Seepage from Snowmelt

With so many homes built over full basements, spring snowmelt is a recurring concern. Rising groundwater presses against foundation walls and floors, seeping through cracks and cold joints into finished and unfinished lower levels alike.

Winter Pipe Freezes

Pipes in crawl spaces, garages, and exterior walls are vulnerable during Denver's cold snaps. A freeze-thaw cycle can split a pipe and release a steady stream of water into walls or ceilings before it's noticed.

Water Heater Failures

Water heaters tucked into basements or utility closets can fail with age — leaking slowly at first, then releasing their full tank. Lower-level installations mean the damage spreads across floors fast.

Summer Storm Intrusion

Heavy summer rain tests roofs, window seals, and grading around the home. Water pooling against the foundation or entering through compromised flashing ends up in the basement or lower walls.

Sewer Backups

Intense rainfall can send wastewater back through basement-level drains and fixtures. Because lower levels are the first to back up, finished basements face the greatest contamination risk.

University Water Damage FAQ

My basement floods every spring. Can providers help with recurring seepage?

Yes. Providers can extract the water, dry the structure, and identify where seepage is entering so you can plan longer-term fixes. Recurring spring moisture is common in south Denver's basement-heavy housing stock.

How long does it take to dry out a flooded basement?

It varies with the amount of water, materials affected, and weather. Many basements dry in several days with commercial equipment, but finished walls and flooring can extend the timeline. The provider will give you an estimate after inspection.

What should I do while waiting for the provider to arrive?

If it's safe, shut off the water source, turn off electricity to affected areas, and move valuables to dry ground. Avoid wading into standing water that may be contaminated or near electrical hazards.

Do providers work on weekends and holidays?

Many independent providers in the network offer 24/7 emergency availability, including weekends and holidays, since water emergencies don't follow a schedule.
